\begin{abstract}
The deployment of autonomous multi-agent swarms operating over shared enterprise transactional databases introduces a fundamental concurrency crisis. Unlike classical database transactions characterized by sub-millisecond compiled SQL queries, autonomous agents exhibit high-latency semantic reasoning phases (250~ms to 2,500~ms per step; 500~ms to 5,000~ms across multi-step workflows) while evaluating state-dependent domain policies, invoking external compliance tools, and executing multi-step LLM prompts within active critical sections. Under standard Strict Two-Phase Locking (S2PL), overlapping multi-resource lock acquisitions generate closed cycles in the system's Wait-For-Graph ($\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}$), while timestamp-based prevention (Wait-Die, Wound-Wait) and Optimistic Concurrency Control (OCC) trigger frequent mid-execution or commit-time aborts. Crucially, in multi-agent swarms, transaction aborts induce severe \textit{livelock token thrashing}, as aborted agents re-execute stochastic planning paths and discard hundreds to thousands of already-billed LLM inference tokens.

To resolve this systems bottleneck within a centralized shared-memory transactional lock coordinator or connection-pool gateway (motivated by single-leader OLTP lock tables such as a Google Cloud Spanner Paxos group leader, AlloyDB/PostgreSQL, or CockroachDB, and evaluated here over an in-memory transactional row-state mirror backed by a persistent SQLite Write-Ahead Logging audit ledger), we present the \textbf{Epistemic Priority Ceiling Protocol (EPCP)}, an autonomous, invariant-preserving, provably deadlock-free concurrency control architecture. EPCP establishes a monotonically latched dynamic priority metric $\tilde{\Pi}_i(t)$ incorporating agent task tree depth, accumulated LLM token investment with aging, and inverse epistemic entropy, paired with a static upper-envelope preemption level $\Pi_i^{\max}$ over exact 64-bit integer lexicographical priority tuples. By combining 64-partition intent-footprint priority ceilings ($\mathcal{F}(T_i) \subseteq \{1, \dots, 64\}$) with incremental in-footprint row locking and a guarded preemptive admission invariant ($\Pi_i^{\text{eff}}(t) > C_{-A_i}^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i))$), EPCP serializes initial admission across intersecting footprints while allowing agents to dynamically discover Step-2 row keys during Step-1 LLM synthesis without upfront row-key pre-declaration or unisolated speculative pre-passes, release Step-1 locks and clear footprint ceilings in $<0.2\text{ ms}$ upon domain pre-check rejection, and execute disjoint footprints in full parallel concurrency. We provide a rigorous inductive proof establishing that $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}(t)$ remains a directed acyclic graph (DAG) under all thread interleavings. We implemented EPCP natively on macOS Apple Silicon over an in-memory row-state mirror backed by a persistent SQLite WAL ledger and benchmarked sustained multi-threaded transactions over an enterprise banking and supply chain schema (10,000 Accounts, 5,000 Inventory Items) under heavy Zipfian access skew ($\theta = 0.85$) across sub-millisecond cryptographic HMAC-SHA256 stress tests, 500~ms--1,500~ms multi-step LLM reasoning benchmarks, 3-table dynamic footprint-expansion tests, and live Google Cloud Vertex AI \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} multi-agent runs against S2PL, 2PL-Wait Promote, Wound-Wait, Wait-Die, OCC, Coarse Shard-Scoped C2PL, Fine-Grained Partition C2PL ($B_{\text{part}}=64$), 64-Partition Canonical Ordered Locking ($B_{\text{part}}=64$), and Row-Level Canonical Ordered Locking. Empirical results demonstrate that EPCP achieves a \textbf{0.00\% deadlock and concurrency abort rate} across all regimes, completely eliminating victim transaction rollbacks while preserving strict conflict serializability and physical ACID integrity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{E}{nterprise} cloud platforms are undergoing a paradigm shift from human-in-the-loop task execution to autonomous multi-agent systems. Built on orchestration frameworks such as Google Agent Development Kit (ADK) and Vertex AI Agent Builder, autonomous agent swarms are delegated end-to-end responsibilities over core enterprise workflows, including supply chain reconciliation, automated loan underwriting, financial clearing, and algorithmic procurement. In these architectures, multiple autonomous agents execute concurrently through a centralized shared-memory transactional lock coordinator or connection-pool gateway. While this gateway architecture is motivated by single-node or single-leader OLTP lock managers such as a Google Cloud Spanner Paxos group leader~\cite{corbett2013spanner}, AlloyDB/PostgreSQL, or CockroachDB (leaving decentralized cross-leader two-phase commit ceiling synchronization across geo-distributed consensus groups to future work), our empirical testbed evaluates the coordinator over a synchronized in-memory transactional row-state mirror backed by a persistent SQLite Write-Ahead Logging (WAL) audit ledger so lock-management primitives are isolated without external network variance.

\subsection{The Semantic Reasoning Latency Expansion and Why Locks Span Inference}
Classical transaction processing was developed under the assumption that transactions are short-lived, pre-compiled SQL queries with execution latencies on the order of 100 microseconds to 5 milliseconds~\cite{gray1992transaction, bernstein1987concurrency}. Under this microsecond regime, locking protocols such as Strict Two-Phase Locking (S2PL)~\cite{eswaran1976notions} provide conflict serializability with negligible lock contention windows.

In autonomous multi-agent architectures, however, transactions are inherently interleaved with \textit{state-dependent semantic reasoning loops}. A natural question is whether holding locks across LLM inference can be avoided altogether via (i) snapshot reads followed by short compare-and-swap (CAS) / Optimistic Concurrency Control (OCC)~\cite{kung1981optimistic} validation at commit, (ii) simple commutative escrow columns (e.g., \texttt{reserved\_amount}), or (iii) Saga-based non-isolated sub-transactions~\cite{chang2025sagallm}. In regulated enterprise workflows, each of these alternatives breaks down under high contention:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Failure of Snapshot-Read + CAS and Scalar Escrow on Non-Commutative State Mutations:} While simple scalar decrements can use commutative escrow counters, enterprise agent workflows perform \textit{non-commutative, multi-attribute structural mutations}---such as corporate loan covenant restructuring, cross-collateralized margin re-indexing, and serialized lot compliance---where Step 2's multi-turn LLM synthesis, AML polynomial risk scoring, and generated SQL/JSON compliance patch depend nonlinearly on the exact vector of row attributes read in Step 1. Specifically, in our banking and inventory schemas, concurrent agents simultaneously read and mutate coupled high-contention attributes---including dynamic credit-tier covenants (\texttt{CustomerTier}, \texttt{CreditLimit}), net liquidity ratios (\texttt{Balance}), and monotonic audit counters (\texttt{AccountVersion}) in \texttt{Accounts}, alongside active reservation ratios (\texttt{StockCount}, \texttt{ReservedCount}) and regulatory lot categories (\texttt{Category}, \texttt{ItemVersion}) in \texttt{Inventory}---all of which directly parameterize $\text{Infer}(\phi)$'s risk-tier classification and counterparty selection, preventing separation of read-mostly semantic attributes from hot scalar escrow fields. When an agent acquires a pessimistic lock on $R_a$, it first runs an immediate sub-microsecond scalar sufficiency pre-filter (\texttt{Biz-Rule Reject}) to fast-exit obviously depleted accounts or out-of-stock SKUs with zero wasted LLM tokens; for funded rows passing this pre-filter, the subsequent $\text{Infer}(\phi)$ phase evaluates the full multi-attribute state of $R_a$. Under snapshot-read + CAS (OCC)~\cite{kung1981optimistic}, if any contending agent modifies $R_a$ during the 500~ms--5,000~ms multi-step reasoning window, the generated compliance patch is invalidated and CAS fails at commit time \textit{after 100\% of the multi-step LLM token budget has already been expended}. Holding a pessimistic lock across $\text{Infer}(\phi)$ protects funded rows from mid-inference invalidation.
    \item \textbf{Prohibition of Uncommitted State Exposure:} Saga-based agent workflows~\cite{chang2025sagallm} release locks early by committing intermediate sub-transactions and relying on reverse compensating actions upon failure. However, in strict double-entry banking and regulatory clearing, exposing transient intermediate states to concurrent readers violates strict serializability and triggers cascading audit exceptions.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, preserving strict ACID isolation without post-hoc validation rollbacks on non-commutative multi-attribute workflows requires holding pessimistic locks across intermediate reasoning phases, expanding lock retention durations from milliseconds to several seconds (500~ms to 5,000~ms)---a $10^3\times$ to $10^4\times$ duration expansion.

\subsection{The Livelock and Token Thrashing Crisis}
When agents acquire locks across multiple resources in dynamic orderings (e.g., Agent $A_1$ acquiring Account $\to$ Inventory while Agent $A_2$ acquires Inventory $\to$ Account), Wait-For-Graph ($\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}$) cycles form rapidly. Under S2PL, deadlock detection modules identify cycles and forcibly abort a ``victim'' transaction~\cite{gray1992transaction}. Similarly, classical timestamp-ordered deadlock prevention schemes~\cite{rosenkrantz1978system, harding2017evaluation} avoid cycles by aborting transactions whenever timestamp inversions occur: under non-preemptive \textit{Wait-Die}, a younger requester immediately ``dies'' (aborts) upon encountering an older lock holder while an older requester waits; under preemptive \textit{Wound-Wait} (used in Google Cloud Spanner~\cite{corbett2013spanner}), an older requester immediately ``wounds'' (preemptively aborts) a younger lock holder while a younger requester waits.

In traditional relational databases, aborting an in-memory SQL transaction incurs minimal penalty~\cite{harding2017evaluation}. In multi-agent swarms, however, aborting an in-flight agent transaction---whether via S2PL deadlock victim selection, Wait-Die self-abort, Wound-Wait preemption~\cite{rosenkrantz1978system}, or OCC validation failure~\cite{kung1981optimistic}---results in \textbf{catastrophic token thrashing}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Token \& Financial Waste:} An agent rolled back mid-workflow loses its in-flight transactional state, discarding hundreds to thousands of already-billed LLM inference tokens (e.g., on Gemini 2.5 Pro / Flash endpoints).
    \item \textbf{Stochastic Lock Retrying:} Upon restart, the agent re-evaluates prompts against mutated state, frequently re-colliding with contending agents.
    \item \textbf{System Saturation:} Under heavy partition skew, abort-based concurrency controllers suffer persistent rollback cascades.
\end{itemize}

Real-time concurrency control offers foundational mechanisms for deadlock-free execution without victim aborts. Priority Inheritance and Priority Ceiling Protocols (PCP) were pioneered by Sha, Rajkumar, and Lehoczky~\cite{sha1990priority} for uniprocessor tasks with static priorities, and extended to dynamic-priority real-time tasks (such as Earliest Deadline First) by Chen and Lin's Dynamic Priority Ceiling Protocol~\cite{chen1990dynamic} and Baker's Stack Resource Policy (SRP)~\cite{baker1991stack}, which decouple static resource preemption levels from dynamic task urgency. In real-time databases, Abbott and Garcia-Molina~\cite{abbott1992scheduling} evaluated priority-cognizant 2PL variants---\textit{2PL-High Priority} (which aborts lower-priority lock holders, inducing token waste) and \textit{2PL-Wait Promote} (which applies priority inheritance upon direct row lock conflicts). Crucially, because \textit{2PL-Wait Promote} lacks a preemptive partition priority ceiling admission gate, transactions still acquire initial locks in opposing orders and suffer multi-resource $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}$ deadlocks. In parallel, classical database deadlock prevention via row-level Conservative 2PL (C2PL)~\cite{bernstein1987concurrency}, canonical ordered row locking, or deterministic scheduling such as Calvin~\cite{thomson2012calvin} requires transactions to either pre-declare and lock every exact row key upfront or sort all target row IDs prior to acquiring any lock. In multi-step agentic workflows, however, the fine-grained target row key $R_b$ in Step 2 (e.g., which specific warehouse lot or counterparty ledger row to debit) is discovered dynamically from Step 1's LLM inference $\text{Infer}(\phi)$, making exact row-level C2PL or upfront canonical row sorting impossible without an unisolated speculative discovery pre-pass.

It is important to separate two orthogonal design axes when routing headers expose a 64-partition intent footprint $\mathcal{F}(T_i) \subseteq \{1, \dots, 64\}$: (i) the \textit{partition-granularity concurrency gain} of decomposing coarse domain shards ($B_{\text{shard}}=8$) into $B_{\text{part}}=64$ intent hash buckets (metadata that Fine-Grained Partition C2PL or 64-Partition Canonical Ordered Locking can also exploit), and (ii) the \textit{incremental row-level locking, epistemic priority inheritance, and dynamic expansion gains} unique to EPCP. Specifically, once $R_a$ is acquired ($\text{Held}(A_i, t) \ne \emptyset$), EPCP's intersecting-footprint ceiling ($C_{-A_k}^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_k))$) gates all partitions in $\mathcal{F}(T_i)$ (including Step 2's partition) against concurrent entry during Step-1 inference---matching the active-execution partition exclusion footprint of 64-partition C2PL (and on a $<0.2\text{ ms}$ \texttt{Biz-Rule Reject}, both release their held locks in $<0.2\text{ ms}$). Relative to 64-partition C2PL and 64-partition Canonical Ordered Locking (both benchmarked directly in Section~V), EPCP's architectural distinction lies along three dimensions: (a) \textit{incremental fine-grained row locking within $\mathcal{F}(T_i)$} without requiring upfront row keys or unisolated speculative discovery pre-passes, (b) \textit{transitive epistemic priority inheritance and live $\tilde{\Pi}_i^{\text{eff}}(t)$ wake-up ordering} (which dispatches waiting agents with the highest accumulated token investment and action certainty first rather than FIFO or static-priority partition queues, and further allows finite-stock workloads to prioritize deeper high-quantity settlements), and (c) \textit{seamless mid-flight out-of-footprint partition expansion} ($\text{part}(R_{\text{req}}) \,\notin\, \mathcal{F}(T_i)$) with SHA-256 \texttt{state\_version\_digest} context-cache replay. Finally, while compensating workflow managers such as SagaLLM~\cite{chang2025sagallm} relax serializability via non-isolated sub-transactions and coarse workload-level adaptive schemes that switch whole transactions between pure OCC~\cite{kung1981optimistic} and pessimistic locking~\cite{harding2017evaluation} remain bounded by the pure OCC and 2PL regimes evaluated in Section~V under stationary high skew ($\theta = 0.85$), neither provides a provably deadlock-free, zero-abort priority ceiling protocol coupled to live LLM token investment and epistemic action entropy.

\subsection{Contributions}
In this work, we present the \textbf{Epistemic Priority Ceiling Protocol (EPCP)}, an autonomous, invariant-preserving (in the concurrency-control sense of maintaining cycle-free wait invariants dynamically without background deadlock detectors or victim aborts) concurrency control architecture engineered for high-latency semantic agent transactions. Our primary contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dual-Level Epistemic Priority Architecture ($\Pi_i^{\max}$ and $\tilde{\Pi}_i(t)$):} A formal priority formulation coupling task delegation depth ($d_i$), clipped cumulative token and aging expenditure ($\Omega_i(t)$), and inverse normalized token-level Shannon action entropy ($1 - \mathcal{H}_i(t)$, where $\mathcal{H}_i(t) \in [0, 1]$ measures action distribution uncertainty), represented as exact 64-bit integer lexicographical tuples to guarantee strict total ordering without floating-point precision loss.
    \item \textbf{Intersecting-Footprint Partition Priority Ceilings ($C_{-A_i}^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i))$):} A 64-partition lock admission invariant where an agent declares an ADK routing-header hash footprint $\mathcal{F}(T_i) \subseteq \{1, \dots, 64\}$ at registration, serializing initial entry across intersecting dynamic footprints ($\mathcal{F}(T_i) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_k) \ne \emptyset$) to prevent Step-2 dynamic-discovery cycles while executing disjoint footprints ($\mathcal{F}(T_i) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_k) = \emptyset$) in parallel concurrency. We explicitly analyze the false-sharing trade-off of $B_{\text{part}} = 64$ (balancing single-register \texttt{uint64\_t} $O(1)$ bitwise-\texttt{AND} efficiency against hash-collision blocking across disjoint rows during inference windows) and isolate EPCP's epistemic scheduling gains from static 64-bucket partitioning.
    \item \textbf{Provable Deadlock Immunity (Theorem 1):} A rigorous inductive proof over unified contention and ceiling wait edges demonstrating that $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}(t)$ remains a directed acyclic graph (DAG) at all times, guaranteeing a 0.00\% deadlock abort rate without victim selection.
    \item \textbf{Bounded Priority Inversion \& Starvation Freedom (Theorems 2 \& 3):} Proofs establishing single-critical-section inversion bounds per disjoint partition component via transitive priority inheritance and staggered-arrival gating, plus unconditional finite-time completion via timed and release-refreshed senior saturation promotion.
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Empirical \& Live Vertex AI Validation:} Multi-threaded benchmarks implemented over an in-memory transactional row-state mirror backed by a persistent SQLite WAL audit ledger on an enterprise banking and supply chain schema (10,000 Accounts, 5,000 Inventory Items) under Zipfian skew ($\theta = 0.85$), spanning sub-millisecond HMAC-SHA256 stress tests (with Zero-Depletion Controls across $K \in \{20, 30, 40\}$), 500~ms--1,500~ms 2-step LLM reasoning benchmarks across 11 protocols and ablations~\cite{eswaran1976notions, abbott1992scheduling, rosenkrantz1978system, kung1981optimistic, bernstein1987concurrency, harding2017evaluation}, 3-table ($|\mathcal{F}(T_i)| = 3$) dynamic out-of-footprint expansion evaluations, and live Google Cloud Vertex AI \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} multi-agent evaluations on a dedicated enterprise Google Cloud Vertex AI environment.
\end{enumerate}

\section{System Model and Problem Formulation}

\subsection{Execution Environment, False-Sharing Trade-offs, and Intent-Scoped Footprints}
We consider a transactional database system $\mathcal{D}$ arbitrated by a centralized shared-memory lock coordinator (motivated by a single Spanner Paxos group leader's local lock table~\cite{corbett2013spanner} and implemented over an in-memory committed row-state mirror backed by SQLite WAL) maintaining a set of shared row-level resources $\mathcal{R} = \{R_1, R_2, \dots, R_M\}$, mapped via a deterministic FNV-1a 64-bit hash function $\text{part} : \mathcal{R} \to \{1, \dots, B_{\text{part}}\}$ ($B_{\text{part}} = 64$) into fine-grained logical lock partitions. Each transaction's partition footprint is represented compactly as a single 64-bit unsigned integer bitmask (\texttt{uint64\_t}), enabling $O(1)$ hardware bitwise-\texttt{AND} footprint intersection checks. A swarm of $K$ autonomous agent worker threads $\mathcal{A} = \{A_1, A_2, \dots, A_K\}$ concurrently execute transactional tasks.

Unlike row-level C2PL (which requires knowing every exact row primary key $R_a, R_b$ before locking), EPCP requires only an \textbf{Intent-Scoped Partition Footprint} $\mathcal{F}(T_i) \subseteq \{1, \dots, B_{\text{part}}\}$ (typically $|\mathcal{F}(T_i)| \in \{2, 3\}$ out of $B_{\text{part}} = 64$), extracted statically from the mandatory routing metadata of the agent's ADK/MCP tool invocation schema (e.g., the 64-bucket hash IDs of the target customer account segment and warehouse SKU category). Mapping $10{,}000$ accounts and $5{,}000$ inventory items onto $B_{\text{part}} = 64$ partitions introduces a deliberate architectural trade-off between hardware bitmask efficiency and \textit{false-sharing partition contention}: two transactions targeting completely disjoint physical rows ($R_a \ne R_{a'}$ and $R_b \ne R_{b'}$) that hash to a common bucket in $\{1, \dots, 64\}$ ($1/64 \approx 1.56\%$ single-bucket collision probability) have intersecting footprints ($\mathcal{F}(T_i) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_k) \ne \emptyset$), causing the second transaction to wait at EPCP's admission gate during the first transaction's reasoning window before acquiring $R_{a'}$ or running its \texttt{Biz-Rule Reject} pre-filter, whereas row-level S2PL admits disjoint rows within the same hash bucket concurrently. We select $B_{\text{part}} = 64$ because a 64-bit word fits in a single native CPU register while keeping random two-partition overlap low enough ($\approx 6.1\%$ for uniform pairs) that eliminating multi-resource deadlocks and token rollbacks yields $+20.3\%\text{--}+22.9\%$ higher committed goodput than row-level S2PL under heavy Zipfian contention ($\theta = 0.85$); deployments requiring still lower false sharing can widen footprints to 256-bit AVX2 or 512-bit AVX-512 bitvectors ($B_{\text{part}} \in \{256, 512\}$) in $O(1)$ SIMD instructions.

While $\mathcal{F}(T_i)$ is known at registration, the exact fine-grained row keys $R_a, R_b \in \mathcal{R}$ within $\mathcal{F}(T_i)$ are discovered and locked incrementally during execution:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tx_model}
\begin{aligned}
T_i = \langle &\text{Register}(\mathcal{F}(T_i)),\, \text{Lock}(R_a),\, \text{BizCheck}(R_a),\, \text{Infer}(\phi), \\
&\text{StageWrite}(R_a),\, \text{Lock}(R_b),\, \dots,\, \text{Commit} \rangle
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\text{Infer}(\phi)$ denotes parameterized LLM policy inference (using $\phi$ to distinguish model parameters from the Zipfian skew parameter $\theta$), and $\text{StageWrite}(R_a)$ stages mutations in a transaction-local write buffer that is only flushed atomically to physical database storage at $\text{Commit}$ after all locks are held. If an agent ever attempts to request an out-of-footprint resource $R_{\text{req}}$ with $\text{part}(R_{\text{req}}) \,\notin\, \mathcal{F}(T_i)$, the coordinator rejects the lock call (returning $\textsc{Restart}$ in Algorithm~\ref{alg:epcp}), discards the uncommitted local write buffer (so zero physical database rollback is required), memoizes completed $\text{Infer}(\phi)$ outputs in the agent's local context cache keyed by a cryptographic SHA-256 digest of the read attributes of $R_a$ (\texttt{state\_version\_digest}), releases all held locks with full waiter wake-up while keeping $A_i \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t)$, expands $\mathcal{F}(T_i) \leftarrow \mathcal{F}(T_i) \cup \{\text{part}(R_{\text{req}})\}$ while preserving $(t_{\text{start},i}, \text{rank}(T_i))$, and unwinds execution to restart from Step 1 ($\text{Lock}(R_a)$). Upon re-acquiring $R_a$, if $R_a$'s read attributes are unchanged (matching \texttt{state\_version\_digest}), the memoized inference output is replayed in microseconds with \textbf{zero re-billed LLM tokens}; if $R_a$ was modified by another transaction during the brief unlock window, the SHA-256 digest misses and $\text{Infer}(\phi)$ is freshly re-evaluated against the new state. Thus, EPCP's zero-token-waste guarantee holds unconditionally whenever declared footprints bound candidate partitions upfront (as in our 2-table ADK schema), and holds conditionally on \texttt{state\_version\_digest} cache hits ($91.4\%$ hit rate at $10\%$ expansion in our 3-table expansion benchmark in Section~V-B) when dynamic footprint expansion occurs.

\subsection{Unified Wait-For-Graph and Deadlock Formalization}
Let $\text{Held}(A_j, t) \subseteq \mathcal{R}$ denote the set of resources currently locked by agent $A_j$ at time $t$.

\begin{definition}[Unified Wait-For-Graph]
The Unified Wait-For-Graph at time $t$, denoted $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}(t) = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}(t))$, is a directed graph where vertices $\mathcal{V} = \mathcal{A}$ represent active agent worker threads, and a directed edge $(A_i \to A_j) \in \mathcal{E}(t)$ ($i \ne j$) exists if and only if agent $A_i$ is currently suspended awaiting lock acquisition on resource $R_{\text{req}}$ ($\text{part}(R_{\text{req}}) \in \mathcal{F}(T_i)$) because:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Direct Contention Wait:} $R_{\text{req}} \in \text{Held}(A_j, t)$; or
    \item \textbf{Ceiling Preemption Wait:} Eq.~\eqref{eq:ceiling_rule} fails for $A_i$, and $A_j \in \mathcal{B}_{\text{ceil}}(A_i, t)$ ($j \ne i$) is an active lock holder ($\text{Held}(A_j, t) \ne \emptyset$) blocking $A_i$'s admission under Section~III-C.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Deadlock State]
A system is in a deadlock state at time $t$ if and only if there exists a directed cycle in $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}(t)$:
\begin{equation}
A_{p_1} \to A_{p_2} \to \dots \to A_{p_L} \to A_{p_1}
\end{equation}
Under S2PL, resolving this cycle requires selecting a victim agent $A_{p_v}$ and aborting its transaction, rolling back uncommitted writes and discarding in-flight LLM reasoning tokens.
\end{definition}

\section{The Epistemic Priority Ceiling Protocol}

\subsection{Monotonically Latched Epistemic Priority Formulation}
Unlike classical real-time operating systems where thread priorities are static integers~\cite{sha1990priority, baker1991stack}, autonomous agent priorities must reflect both the economic sunk cost and epistemic certainty of in-flight reasoning tasks while remaining strictly ordered and non-decreasing during an active transaction. Following Baker's SRP principle~\cite{baker1991stack}, EPCP maintains two complementary priority quantities per transaction $T_i$: (1) a static \textbf{Transaction Upper-Envelope Preemption Level} $\Pi_i^{\max}$ (determined at registration by task depth $d_i$ and arrival rank $\text{rank}(T_i)$, and upgraded only upon senior saturation horizon $T_{\text{sat}}$) that defines partition ceilings so ceilings never shift mid-lock and cause self-deadlock, and (2) a live \textbf{Monotonically Latched Dynamic Priority} $\tilde{\Pi}_i(t)$ (driven by real-time token expenditure $\text{Tokens}_i(t)$, wait-time aging, and action certainty $1 - \mathcal{H}_i(t)$) that orders condition-variable wake-up queues and dynamic urgency scheduling among waiting agents. Thus, when a lock holder commits and signals blocked waiters, the waiting agent with the highest live epistemic investment $\tilde{\Pi}_k^{\text{eff}}(t)$ is dispatched first to acquire the released resource.

\begin{definition}[Dynamic and Latched Epistemic Priority]
At time $t$, the instantaneous epistemic score $\Pi_i(t) \in \mathbb{R}^+$ of agent $A_i$ executing transaction $T_i$ (started at $t_{\text{start},i}$) is:
\begin{equation}
\Pi_i(t) \triangleq \Pi_{\text{base}} + w_d \cdot d_i + w_\Omega \cdot \ln(1 + \Omega_i(t)) + w_c \cdot (1 - \mathcal{H}_i(t))
\end{equation}
where $\Pi_{\text{base}} = 1.0$, $d_i \in \{1, \dots, d_{\max}\}$ ($d_{\max} = 4$) is the agent call-graph delegation depth, and $\Omega_i(t)$ combines cumulative LLM token investment with linear wait-time aging ($\gamma_{\text{age}} = 0.05\text{ s}^{-1}$, explicitly clipped at $\Omega_{\max} = 10^4$):
\begin{equation}
\Omega_i(t) \triangleq \min\!\left(\Omega_{\max},\, \frac{\text{Tokens}_i(t)}{100} + \gamma_{\text{age}}(t - t_{\text{start},i})\right)
\end{equation}
with $\Omega_i(t) \in [0, \Omega_{\max}]$. The normalized token-level Shannon entropy $\mathcal{H}_i(t) \in [0, 1]$ over the agent's candidate tool-action distribution $p_{i,t}(v)$ ($v \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}$, extracted from the softmax log-probabilities of the LLM's structured tool-call head at time $t$, initialized to neutral uncertainty $\mathcal{H}_i(t_{\text{start},i}) = 0.5$ prior to the first inference step) is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{H}_i(t) \triangleq \begin{cases}
0, & \text{if } |\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}| \le 1, \\
-\frac{1}{\ln |\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}|}\sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}} p_{i,t}(v)\ln p_{i,t}(v), & \text{if } |\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}| > 1,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with weights fixed at $w_d = 2.0, w_\Omega = 1.5, w_c = 3.0$ (chosen so one delegation level $w_d = 2.0$ outweighs cold-start entropy variance while full epistemic certainty $w_c = 3.0$ and log-token investment $w_\Omega \ln(1+\Omega_i(t)) \in [0, 13.82]$ dominate mid-flight urgency; sensitivity sweeps across $\pm 50\%$ weight perturbations preserve $0.00\%$ aborts with $< 2.1\%$ goodput variation). Furthermore, to guarantee unconditional starvation freedom across arbitrary depths (Theorem 3), if a waiting transaction's age reaches the senior saturation horizon $T_{\text{sat}}$ (defaulting to $\Omega_{\max}/\gamma_{\text{age}} = 2\times 10^5\text{ s}$ as an asymptotic starvation safeguard, or configurable via \texttt{senior\_saturation\_sec} as an independent wall-clock SLA horizon such as $T_{\text{sat}} \in [5\text{ s}, 60\text{ s}]$ decoupled from $\Omega_{\max}$), its latched score AND its upper-envelope preemption score are atomically promoted to the uniform saturation ceiling $\Pi_{\text{sat}} \triangleq \Pi_{\text{base}} + w_d \cdot d_{\max} + w_\Omega \cdot \ln(1 + \Omega_{\max}) + w_c$, updating $\Pi(P_m, t)$ for all $P_m \in \mathcal{F}(T_i)$ and immediately propagating the promoted priority to any blocking holders via Eqs.~\eqref{eq:inheritance}--\eqref{eq:inheritance_dyn}.

Let $\Pi_i^{\text{score}}(t) \triangleq \max_{\tau \in [t_{\text{start},i}, t]} \Pi_i(\tau)$ denote the monotonically latched dynamic score and $\Pi_{i,\max}^{\text{score}} \triangleq \Pi_{\text{base}} + w_d d_i + w_\Omega \ln(1 + \Omega_{\max}) + w_c$ (upgraded to $\Pi_{\text{sat}}$ if $t - t_{\text{start},i} \ge T_{\text{sat}}$) denote the upper-envelope preemption score. To eliminate IEEE-754 floating-point mantissa precision loss or scalar underflow as the monotonic arrival sequence ID $\text{rank}(T_i) \in \mathbb{N}$ grows over unbounded non-idle streams, the coordinator represents and compares priorities internally as exact 64-bit integer lexicographical pairs $\tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_i(t) = \big(\lfloor 10^6 \Pi_i^{\text{score}}(t)\rfloor,\, -\text{rank}(T_i)\big)$ and $\mathbf{\Pi}_i^{\max} = \big(\lfloor 10^6 \Pi_{i,\max}^{\text{score}}\rfloor,\, -\text{rank}(T_i)\big) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0} \times \mathbb{Z}_{< 0}$ (and rebases $\text{rank}(T_i) \leftarrow 1$ only when $\mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t) = \emptyset$; expanding transactions remain in $\mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t)$ during re-registration so $\text{rank}(T_i)$ is never rebased mid-flight). In scalar notation, we write this strict lexicographical tuple order symbolically via the tie-breaker $\epsilon_{\text{tie}} = 10^{-7}$ (where $\Pi^{\text{score}} - \epsilon_{\text{tie}}\text{rank}(T_i)$ is compact symbolic shorthand for the exact 64-bit integer tuple comparison $(\lfloor 10^6 \Pi^{\text{score}} \rfloor, -\text{rank}(T_i))$, rather than literal floating-point subtraction) as the \textbf{Monotonically Latched Dynamic Priority} $\tilde{\Pi}_i(t)$ and the \textbf{Transaction Upper-Envelope Preemption Level} $\Pi_i^{\max}$:
\begin{align}
\tilde{\Pi}_i(t) &\triangleq \Pi_i^{\text{score}}(t) - \epsilon_{\text{tie}} \cdot \text{rank}(T_i), \\
\Pi_i^{\max} &\triangleq \Pi_{i,\max}^{\text{score}} - \epsilon_{\text{tie}} \cdot \text{rank}(T_i)
\end{align}
By construction, for all $t_1 \le t_2$ during $T_i$'s execution, $0 < \tilde{\Pi}_i(t_1) \le \tilde{\Pi}_i(t_2) \le \Pi_i^{\max}$, and $\Pi_i^{\max} \ne \Pi_j^{\max}$ for all $i \ne j$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Atomic Registration and Intersecting-Footprint Partition Ceilings}
Let $\mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t)$ denote the set of registered active agent threads at time $t$. In EPCP, $\text{Register}(\mathcal{F}(T_q))$ and $A_q$'s initial lock check $\text{Lock}(R_a)$ execute as a \textbf{single atomic critical section under the coordinator mutex}: when $A_q$ (executing $T_q$) arrives at time $t_{\text{arr}}$ with declared footprint $\mathcal{F}(T_q)$, $A_q$ is inserted into $\mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t)$ and into $\mathcal{U}(P_m, t) \triangleq \{A_r \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t) \mid P_m \in \mathcal{F}(T_r)\}$ for all $P_m \in \mathcal{F}(T_q)$ (elevating each partition ceiling $\Pi(P_m, t)$ via the exact 64-bit integer tuple comparison $\mathbf{\Pi}_q^{\max}$), and immediately evaluates Eq.~\eqref{eq:ceiling_rule} for $R_a$. If Eq.~\eqref{eq:ceiling_rule} fails because an active lock holder $A_k$ ($\text{Held}(A_k, t_{\text{arr}}) \ne \emptyset$) has an intersecting footprint $\mathcal{F}(T_k) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_q) \ne \emptyset$ (or blocks a higher-priority waiter on $\mathcal{F}(T_q)$), $A_q$ atomically binds wait edges $(A_q \to A_k)$ for all $A_k \in \mathcal{B}_{\text{ceil}}(A_q, t_{\text{arr}})$ (defined in Section~III-C) and propagates $\Pi_q^{\max}$ to $A_k$ via transitive priority inheritance (Eqs.~\eqref{eq:inheritance}--\eqref{eq:inheritance_dyn}) before releasing the coordinator mutex to sleep on its condition variable $\text{CV}(A_q)$.

\begin{definition}[Partition Ceiling and Intersecting-Footprint System Ceiling]
For each logical partition $P_m \in \{1, \dots, B_{\text{part}}\}$, the partition priority ceiling $\Pi(P_m, t)$ (and resource ceiling $C(R_j, t) \triangleq \Pi(\text{part}(R_j), t)$) is the maximum upper-envelope preemption bound among all active agents registered for partition $P_m$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:part_ceiling_def}
\Pi(P_m, t) \triangleq \max \Big( \{0\} \cup \big\{ \Pi_q^{\max} \;\big|\; A_q \in \mathcal{U}(P_m, t) \big\} \Big)
\end{equation}
Because $\Pi(P_m, t)$ is computed over the upper-envelope $\Pi_q^{\max}$ of registered agents, $\Pi(P_m, t) \ge \tilde{\Pi}_q(\tau)$ holds for every $A_q \in \mathcal{U}(P_m, t)$ at all instants $\tau$. For an agent $A_i$ with footprint $\mathcal{F}(T_i)$, let $\mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{hold}}(A_i, t) \triangleq \{A_k \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t) \setminus \{A_i\} \mid \text{Held}(A_k, t) \ne \emptyset \;\land\; \mathcal{F}(T_k) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_i) \ne \emptyset\}$ denote the set of active external lock-holding agents whose declared partition footprint intersects $\mathcal{F}(T_i)$. Furthermore, to prevent a newly arriving lower-priority transaction from cutting ahead of a higher-priority (or $T_{\text{sat}}$-promoted) multi-partition waiter $A_q$ queued at the admission gate---including during the Mesa condition-variable wake-up handoff window between $\textsc{ReleaseAllLocks}$ clearing incoming wait edges and the woken waiter $A_q$ re-acquiring the coordinator mutex---let $\mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{queue}}(A_i, t) \triangleq \{A_k \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t) \setminus \{A_i\} \mid \text{Held}(A_i, t) = \emptyset \;\land\; \text{Held}(A_k, t) \ne \emptyset \;\land\; \exists A_q \in \text{Waiters}(A_k, t) \text{ s.t. } \mathcal{F}(T_q) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_i) \ne \emptyset \land (\mathbf{\Pi}_q^{\text{eff}}(t) > \mathbf{\Pi}_i^{\text{eff}}(t) \lor \tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_q^{\text{eff}}(t) > \tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_i^{\text{eff}}(t))\}$ (where $\Pi^{\text{eff}}(t)$ and $\tilde{\Pi}^{\text{eff}}(t)$ are defined in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:inheritance}--\eqref{eq:inheritance_dyn}) denote the active lock holders blocking a higher-priority waiter on an intersecting partition, let $\mathcal{W}_{\text{pend}}(A_i, t) \triangleq \{A_q \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t) \setminus \{A_i\} \mid \text{Held}(A_i, t) = \emptyset \;\land\; \text{Held}(A_q, t) = \emptyset \;\land\; A_q \text{ is queued at } \text{CV}(A_q) \;\land\; \mathcal{F}(T_q) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_i) \ne \emptyset \;\land\; (\mathbf{\Pi}_q^{\text{eff}}(t) > \mathbf{\Pi}_i^{\text{eff}}(t) \lor \tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_q^{\text{eff}}(t) > \tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_i^{\text{eff}}(t))\}$ denote higher-priority pending waiters on an intersecting partition, and define $\mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}(A_i, t) \triangleq \mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{hold}}(A_i, t) \cup \mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{queue}}(A_i, t)$. The \textbf{External Intersecting-Footprint System Ceiling} $C_{-A_i}^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i))$ and the \textbf{Active-Holder Intersecting-Footprint System Ceiling} $C^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i))$ are defined as:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:ext_ceiling_def}
C_{-A_i}^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i)) &\triangleq \max \Big( \{0\} \nonumber \\
&\quad \cup \big\{ \Pi(P_m, t) \;\big|\; A_k \in \mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{hold}}(A_i, t), \nonumber \\
&\qquad P_m \in \mathcal{F}(T_i) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_k) \big\} \nonumber \\
&\quad \cup \big\{ \Pi(P_m, t) \;\big|\; \text{Held}(A_i, t) = \emptyset, \nonumber \\
&\qquad \exists A_k \in \mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{queue}}(A_i, t),\; \exists A_q \in \text{Waiters}(A_k, t) \nonumber \\
&\qquad\quad \lor\; A_q \in \mathcal{W}_{\text{pend}}(A_i, t), \nonumber \\
&\qquad P_m \in \mathcal{F}(T_i) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_q) \big\} \Big), \\
\label{eq:overall_ceiling_def}
C^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i)) &\triangleq \max \Big( \{0\} \nonumber \\
&\quad \cup \big\{ \Pi_k^{\text{eff}}(t) \;\big|\; A_k \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t), \nonumber \\
&\qquad \text{Held}(A_k, t) \ne \emptyset, \nonumber \\
&\qquad \mathcal{F}(T_i) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_k) \ne \emptyset \big\} \Big)
\end{align}
Notice three fundamental properties of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ext_ceiling_def}--\eqref{eq:overall_ceiling_def}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Parallel Concurrency Across Disjoint 64-Bucket Footprints ($\mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}(A_i, t) = \emptyset \land \mathcal{W}_{\text{pend}}(A_i, t) = \emptyset$):} Whenever no external lock-holding transaction (or higher-priority queued waiter) has a footprint intersecting $\mathcal{F}(T_i)$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ext_ceiling_def} takes the maximum over the empty set and yields $C_{-A_i}^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i)) = 0 < \Pi_i^{\text{eff}}(t)$. Thus, $A_i$ never self-blocks on its own registration in $\mathcal{U}(P_m, t)$ and enters immediately in parallel with all disjoint-footprint transactions.
    \item \textbf{Intersecting-Footprint Admission Exclusion ($\mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}(A_i, t) \ne \emptyset$):} Because $A_i \in \mathcal{U}(P_m, t)$ for all $P_m \in \mathcal{F}(T_i)$, whenever an external transaction $A_k \in \mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{hold}}(A_i, t)$ already holds any lock ($\text{Held}(A_k, t) \ne \emptyset$) and shares at least one partition $P_{\text{shared}} \in \mathcal{F}(T_i) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_k)$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ext_ceiling_def} yields $C_{-A_i}^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i)) \ge \Pi(P_{\text{shared}}, t) \ge \max(\Pi_i^{\max}, \Pi_k^{\max}) \ge \Pi_i^{\max}$. Because Step-2 row keys inside $\mathcal{F}(T_i)$ are discovered dynamically during Step-1 LLM inference, this enforces \textbf{single-holder admission exclusion across intersecting footprints}: a newly arriving $A_i$ ($\text{Held}(A_i, t) = \emptyset$, even if $\Pi_i^{\max} > \Pi_k^{\max}$) is suspended at the admission gate on $\text{CV}(A_i)$ rather than entering $P_{\text{shared}}$ concurrently with $A_k$ (which would risk $A_i$ and $A_k$ dynamically discovering and requesting each other's held rows in Step 2), and instead immediately elevates $A_k$ via transitive priority inheritance ($\Pi_k^{\text{eff}}(t) \leftarrow \max(\Pi_k^{\text{eff}}(t), \Pi_i^{\max})$) so $A_k$ completes its remaining steps rapidly and hands the footprint to $A_i$.
    \item \textbf{Unconditional Step-2 Pass-Through for Active Lock Holders ($\text{Held}(A_i, t) \ne \emptyset$):} Because footprints never expand while locks are held (Algorithm~\ref{alg:epcp} Line 6 releases all held locks prior to footprint expansion), single-holder admission exclusion across intersecting footprints implies by induction over lock-acquisition events that active lock holders have pairwise disjoint footprints at all times $t$:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:disjoint_active_holders}
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Held}(A_i, t) \ne \emptyset &\implies \mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}(A_i, t) = \emptyset \\
    &\implies C_{-A_i}^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i)) = 0 < \Pi_i^{\text{eff}}(t) \\
    &\quad\;\; \land\; C^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i)) = \Pi_i^{\text{eff}}(t).
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    Thus, once $A_i$ holds its Step-1 lock ($\text{Held}(A_i, t) \ne \emptyset$), both disjuncts of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ceiling_rule} are satisfied simultaneously when $A_i$ requests its Step-2 locks within $\mathcal{F}(T_i)$, guaranteeing zero secondary ceiling self-blocking even if an unadmitted multi-partition waiter $A_M$ is registered on one of $\mathcal{F}(T_i)$'s partitions while blocked on a disjoint lock holder.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\subsection{EPCP Resource Allocation and Guarded Pass-Through Rules}
When agent $A_i$ requests lock acquisition on resource $R_{\text{req}}$ ($\text{part}(R_{\text{req}}) \in \mathcal{F}(T_i)$) at time $t$ (summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:epcp}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Reentrancy:} If $R_{\text{req}} \in \text{Held}(A_i, t)$, access is granted immediately.
    \item \textbf{Ceiling Admissibility \& Guarded Inheritance Pass-Through Rule:} If $R_{\text{req}}$ is currently free, $A_i$ is granted the lock \textbf{if and only if}:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:ceiling_rule}
    \begin{aligned}
    \Pi_i^{\text{eff}}(t) &> C_{-A_i}^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i)) \\
    &\lor \Big( \text{Held}(A_i, t) \ne \emptyset \;\land\; \Pi_i^{\text{eff}}(t) = C^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i)) \Big)
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    When $\text{Held}(A_i, t) = \emptyset$ (initial admission), the second disjunct is false and $A_i$ must satisfy the strict inequality $\Pi_i^{\text{eff}}(t) > C_{-A_i}^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i))$, which holds if and only if $\mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}(A_i, t) = \emptyset$ and $\mathcal{W}_{\text{pend}}(A_i, t) = \emptyset$. Conversely, once $\text{Held}(A_i, t) \ne \emptyset$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:disjoint_active_holders} guarantees that $C_{-A_i}^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i)) = 0 < \Pi_i^{\text{eff}}(t)$ (satisfying the first disjunct directly) and $\Pi_i^{\text{eff}}(t) = C^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i))$ (satisfying the second disjunct), permitting $A_i$ to acquire all remaining Step-2 locks in $\mathcal{F}(T_i)$ without self-blocking.
    \item \textbf{Unified Contention \& Ceiling Suspension:} If $R_{\text{req}}$ is held by $A_{\text{hold}} \ne A_i$ or Eq.~\eqref{eq:ceiling_rule} fails (both tested in Rule 2), let $\mathcal{B}_{\text{ceil}}(A_i, t) \triangleq \{A_j \in \mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}(A_i, t) \mid \max_{P_m \in \mathcal{F}(T_i) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_j)} \Pi(P_m, t) \ge \Pi_i^{\text{eff}}(t)\} \cup \{A_j \in \mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{queue}}(A_i, t)\}$ denote the set of external ceiling-blocking lock holders. On each wait iteration, $A_i$ first clears any stale outgoing edges $\mathcal{E}(A_i) \leftarrow \emptyset$ (recomputing Eqs.~\eqref{eq:inheritance}--\eqref{eq:inheritance_dyn} for any former blockers), binds directed wait edges $(A_i \to A_j)$ in $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}(t)$ for all $A_j \in (\{A_{\text{hold}}\} \cup \mathcal{B}_{\text{ceil}}(A_i, t))$ (so every intersecting lock holder is elevated simultaneously), propagates $\Pi_i^{\text{eff}}(t)$ and $\tilde{\Pi}_i^{\text{eff}}(t)$ transitively via Eqs.~\eqref{eq:inheritance}--\eqref{eq:inheritance_dyn}, and suspends on its per-agent condition variable $\text{CV}(A_i)$ with timeout $\Delta t_{\text{wait}} = \infty \text{ if promoted to } \Pi_{\text{sat}} \text{ else } \max(0, T_{\text{sat}} - (t - t_{\text{start},i}))$ (so an already-promoted waiter awaits explicit release notification without zero-timeout busy-spinning).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{EPCP Atomic Lock Acquisition \& Release}
\label{alg:epcp}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\small
\STATE \textbf{Procedure} $\textsc{AcquireLock}(A_i, R_{\text{req}})$ \textbf{under Coordinator Mutex:}
\IF{$A_i \,\notin\, \mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t)$}
    \STATE Insert $A_i$ into $\mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t)$ and $\mathcal{U}(P_m, t)$ ($\forall P_m \in \mathcal{F}(T_i)$); update $\Pi(P_m, t)$
\ENDIF
\IF{$\text{part}(R_{\text{req}}) \,\notin\, \mathcal{F}(T_i)$}
    \STATE Discard local staged writes; memoize $\text{Infer}(\phi)$ by SHA-256 \texttt{state\_version\_digest}; release $\text{Held}(A_i, t)$, clear incoming $(A_k \to A_i) \in \mathcal{E}(t)$, rebind multi-partition waiters to remaining active holders, reset $(\Pi_i^{\text{eff}}(t), \tilde{\Pi}_i^{\text{eff}}(t)) \leftarrow (\Pi_i^{\max}, \tilde{\Pi}_i(t))$, refresh age-dependent $\tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_k(t)$ and $T_{\text{sat}}$ promotions, and wake waiters in descending order of $\tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_k^{\text{eff}}(t)$ while keeping $A_i \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t)$; expand $\mathcal{F}(T_i) \leftarrow \mathcal{F}(T_i) \cup \{\text{part}(R_{\text{req}})\}$ preserving $(t_{\text{start},i}, \text{rank}(T_i))$; insert $A_i \in \mathcal{U}(\text{part}(R_{\text{req}}), t)$ and update $\Pi(\text{part}(R_{\text{req}}), t)$; \RETURN $\textsc{Restart}$
\ENDIF
\LOOP
    \IF{$t - t_{\text{start},i} \ge T_{\text{sat}}$}
        \STATE Promote $\tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_i(t), \mathbf{\Pi}_i^{\max} \leftarrow (\lfloor 10^6 \Pi_{\text{sat}} \rfloor, -\text{rank}(T_i))$; update $\Pi(P_m, t)$ and propagate $\Pi_i^{\text{eff}}(t), \tilde{\Pi}_i^{\text{eff}}(t)$ along $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}(t)$ via Eqs.~\eqref{eq:inheritance}--\eqref{eq:inheritance_dyn}
    \ENDIF
    \IF{$R_{\text{req}} \in \text{Held}(A_i, t)$}
        \RETURN \TRUE
    \ENDIF
    \STATE Compute $C_{-A_i}^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i))$ and $C^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_i))$ via 64-bit footprint masks
    \IF{$R_{\text{req}}$ is free \AND Eq.~\eqref{eq:ceiling_rule} holds}
        \STATE $\text{Held}(A_i, t) \leftarrow \text{Held}(A_i, t) \cup \{R_{\text{req}}\}$; clear outgoing $\mathcal{E}(A_i)$ and recompute former blockers; \RETURN \TRUE
    \ENDIF
    \STATE Clear stale outgoing $\mathcal{E}(A_i) \leftarrow \emptyset$ (recomputing Eqs.~\eqref{eq:inheritance}--\eqref{eq:inheritance_dyn} for former blockers); bind $(A_i \to A_j) \in \mathcal{E}(t)$ for all $A_j \in (\{\text{holder}(R_{\text{req}})\} \cup \mathcal{B}_{\text{ceil}}(A_i, t))$
    \STATE Propagate $\Pi_i^{\text{eff}}(t)$ and $\tilde{\Pi}_i^{\text{eff}}(t)$ transitively along $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}(t)$ via Eqs.~\eqref{eq:inheritance}--\eqref{eq:inheritance_dyn}
    \STATE $\textsc{Wait}\big(\text{CV}(A_i),\, \Delta t_{\text{wait}} = \infty \text{ if promoted to } \Pi_{\text{sat}} \text{ else } \max(0, T_{\text{sat}} - (t - t_{\text{start},i}))\big)$
\ENDLOOP
\STATE \textbf{Procedure} $\textsc{ReleaseAllLocks}(A_j)$ \textbf{at Commit / Biz-Rule Exit:}
\STATE Flush staged writes (if committing); release $\text{Held}(A_j, t)$; remove $A_j$ from $\mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t)$, clear incoming edges $(A_k \to A_j) \in \mathcal{E}(t)$ incident to $A_j$, rebind sleeping multi-partition waiters to any remaining active blockers, and update $\Pi(P_m, t)$; refresh age-dependent $\tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_k(t)$ and pending $T_{\text{sat}}$ promotions across waiting candidates (propagating along remaining edges in $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}(t)$); wake waiters on $\text{CV}(A_k)$ in descending order of $\tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_k^{\text{eff}}(t)$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Unified Transitive Epistemic Priority Inheritance and Complexity Bound}
To prevent unbounded priority inversion under both direct contention and ceiling preemption, let $\text{Waiters}(A_j, t) \triangleq \{A_k \in \mathcal{A} \mid (A_k \to A_j) \in \mathcal{E}(t)\}$ denote all agents blocked by resources held by $A_j$. The effective preemption priority $\Pi_j^{\text{eff}}(t)$ and effective dynamic urgency $\tilde{\Pi}_j^{\text{eff}}(t)$ of $A_j$ are maintained atomically:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:inheritance}
\Pi_j^{\text{eff}}(t) &\triangleq \max \left( \Pi_j^{\max},\, \max_{A_k \in \text{Waiters}(A_j, t)} \Pi_k^{\text{eff}}(t) \right), \\
\label{eq:inheritance_dyn}
\tilde{\Pi}_j^{\text{eff}}(t) &\triangleq \max \left( \tilde{\Pi}_j(t),\, \max_{A_k \in \text{Waiters}(A_j, t)} \tilde{\Pi}_k^{\text{eff}}(t) \right)
\end{align}
Whenever $A_i$ blocks on $A_j \in (\{\text{holder}(R_{\text{req}})\} \cup \mathcal{B}_{\text{ceil}}(A_i, t))$, $A_j$ immediately inherits both $\Pi_i^{\text{eff}}(t)$ and $\tilde{\Pi}_i^{\text{eff}}(t)$. Upon transaction commit (or clean \texttt{Biz-Rule Reject} exit), $A_j$ atomically releases all locks in $\text{Held}(A_j, t)$, removes itself from $\mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t)$---deleting $A_j$ from $\mathcal{U}(P_m, t)$ and updating each partition max-heap $\Pi(P_m, t)$ for $P_m \in \mathcal{F}(T_j)$---removes all incoming edges $(A_k \to A_j) \in \mathcal{E}(t)$ incident to $A_j$, rebinds any multi-partition waiter $A_k$ to remaining active lock holders intersecting $\mathcal{F}(T_k)$, refreshes age-dependent waiter scores $\tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_k(t)$ and any pending $T_{\text{sat}}$ promotions (propagating along remaining wait edges), and signals waiting threads on $\text{CV}(A_k)$ in descending order of live dynamic urgency $\tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_k^{\text{eff}}(t)$. Crucially, because Eq.~\eqref{eq:disjoint_active_holders} guarantees that active lock holders ($\text{Held}(A_j, t) \ne \emptyset$) have pairwise disjoint footprints and never block on secondary locks within $\mathcal{F}(T_j)$, $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}(t)$ is strictly a \textbf{bipartite DAG of maximum path length $1$} (sleeping waiters with $\text{Held} = \emptyset$ and in-degree $0$ pointing directly to active lock holders with $\text{Held} \ne \emptyset$ and out-degree $0$). Consequently, priority propagation at Line 22 traverses at most $|\mathcal{B}_{\text{ceil}}(A_i, t)| \le \min(B_{\text{part}}, K - 1) < K$ single-hop edges in $\mathcal{O}(K)$ time (and waking waiters re-evaluate ceilings in $\mathcal{O}(K)$ time via cached \texttt{uint64\_t} waiter footprint masks), bounding each lock acquisition to $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{F}(T_i)|\log K + K)$ time and each unlock with sorted waiter wake-up to $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{F}(T_i)|\log K + K\log K)$ time ($< 1.8\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ at $K=40, |\mathcal{F}(T_i)| \le 3$).

\section{Formal Theoretical Analysis}

\subsection{Theorem 1: Deadlock Elimination via Inductive Dynamic Acyclicity}
\begin{theorem}[Deadlock Elimination]
Under the Epistemic Priority Ceiling Protocol (EPCP), the Unified Wait-For-Graph $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}(t)$ is a directed acyclic graph (DAG) for all times $t \ge 0$. Consequently, deadlocks are provably impossible, and the transaction abort rate due to deadlock is identically 0.00\%.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We prove Theorem 1 by contradiction using mathematical induction over the discrete sequence of lock acquisition and suspension events $0 = t_0 < t_1 < t_2 < \dots < t_N$. Throughout this section, we write $\Pi_q^{\max}(t)$ for $A_q$'s instantaneous upper-envelope preemption level at time $t$ (equal to its initial $\Pi_q^{\max}$ for $t - t_{\text{start},q} < T_{\text{sat}}$ and promoted to $(\lfloor 10^6 \Pi_{\text{sat}} \rfloor, -\text{rank}(T_q))$ for $t - t_{\text{start},q} \ge T_{\text{sat}}$).

\textbf{Base Case ($t=0$):} At system initialization, no locks are held and $\mathcal{E}(0) = \emptyset$, which is trivially acyclic.

\textbf{Inductive Hypothesis:} Assume that for all $t < t_k$, $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}(t)$ is a directed acyclic graph (DAG).

\textbf{Inductive Step ($t = t_k$):} Suppose for the sake of contradiction that at event time $t_k$, a lock request by some agent closes a minimal directed cycle of length $L \ge 2$ in $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}(t_k)$:
\begin{equation}
A_{p_1} \to A_{p_2} \to \dots \to A_{p_L} \to A_{p_1}
\end{equation}
where $\text{succ}(m) \triangleq (m \bmod L) + 1$. By Definition 1, every directed edge $(A_{p_m} \to A_{p_{\text{succ}(m)}}) \in \mathcal{E}(t_k)$ requires that $A_{p_{\text{succ}(m)}}$ is currently holding at least one lock ($\text{Held}(A_{p_{\text{succ}(m)}}, t_k) \ne \emptyset$). Since every cycle vertex $A_{p_m}$ has an incoming edge from $A_{p_{(m-2 \bmod L)+1}}$, every $A_{p_m}$ satisfies $\text{Held}(A_{p_m}, t_k) \ne \emptyset$. Because $\mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{queue}}(A_i, t)$ is gated by $\text{Held}(A_i, t) = \emptyset$, $\text{Held}(A_{p_m}, t_k) \ne \emptyset$ immediately implies $\mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{queue}}(A_{p_m}, t_k) = \emptyset$, so any edge $(A_{p_m} \to A_{p_{\text{succ}(m)}}) \in \mathcal{E}(t_k)$ must arise from $\mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{hold}}(A_{p_m}, t_k)$ (or direct row contention within declared footprints) and therefore requires direct footprint intersection:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:footprint_overlap}
\mathcal{F}(T_{p_m}) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_{p_{\text{succ}(m)}}) \ne \emptyset, \quad \forall m \in \{1, \dots, L\}
\end{equation}
(Note that Eq.~\eqref{eq:disjoint_active_holders} already implies that active lock holders have pairwise disjoint footprints and out-degree $0$; the derivation below proves from first principles why no active lock holder could ever have entered an intersecting footprint to form Eq.~\eqref{eq:footprint_overlap}.)

For each agent $A_{p_m}$ in the cycle, since $\text{Held}(A_{p_m}, t_k) \ne \emptyset$, let $t_{p_m}^{\text{entry}} < t_k$ denote the \textit{most recent} instant prior to $t_k$ when $A_{p_m}$ transitioned from holding no locks ($\text{Held}(A_{p_m}, t_{p_m}^{\text{entry}-}) = \emptyset$) to acquiring the first of its currently held locks $R_{(p_m)}^{\text{first}}$ (guaranteeing that $\text{Held}(A_{p_m}, t) \ne \emptyset$ held continuously throughout $[t_{p_m}^{\text{entry}}, t_k]$ without any intervening lock release or footprint expansion). Because events are serialized by the coordinator mutex, entry timestamps are distinct; let $A_{p^*}$ ($p^* \in \{1, \dots, L\}$) be the unique agent in the cycle that entered its current critical section latest in time:
\begin{equation}
t^* \triangleq t_{p^*}^{\text{entry}} = \max_{m \in \{1, \dots, L\}} t_{p_m}^{\text{entry}} < t_k
\end{equation}
Consider the system state at $t^{*-}$ immediately prior to $A_{p^*}$ being granted $R_{(p^*)}^{\text{first}}$. Because $t_{p_m}^{\text{entry}} < t^*$ for every other cycle member $m \ne p^*$, $A_{p^*}$'s cycle successor $A_{p_{\text{succ}(p^*)}}$ ($\text{succ}(p^*) \ne p^*$ since $L \ge 2$) had already entered its critical section and was actively holding at least $R_{(p_{\text{succ}(p^*))}}^{\text{first}}$ at $t^{*-}$, so $\text{Held}(A_{p_{\text{succ}(p^*)}}, t^{*-}) \ne \emptyset$.

By Eq.~\eqref{eq:footprint_overlap}, there exists at least one shared partition $P_{\text{shared}} \in \mathcal{F}(T_{p^*}) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_{p_{\text{succ}(p^*)}})$. By the atomic registration invariant (Section~III-B), both $A_{p^*}$ and $A_{p_{\text{succ}(p^*)}}$ belonged to $\mathcal{U}(P_{\text{shared}}, t^{*-})$ at $t^{*-}$. Furthermore, because Lines 10--12 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:epcp} evaluate any pending $T_{\text{sat}}$ promotion and update both $\Pi_{p^*}^{\max}(t^{*-})$ and $\Pi(P_m, t^{*-})$ under the coordinator mutex \textit{prior} to evaluating Eq.~\eqref{eq:ceiling_rule} at Line 17, the partition priority ceiling of $P_{\text{shared}}$ at $t^{*-}$ satisfied:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Pi(P_{\text{shared}}, t^{*-}) &= \max_{A_q \in \mathcal{U}(P_{\text{shared}}, t^{*-})} \Pi_q^{\max}(t^{*-}) \\
&\ge \max\big(\Pi_{p^*}^{\max}(t^{*-}),\, \Pi_{p_{\text{succ}(p^*)}}^{\max}(t^{*-})\big) \\
&\ge \Pi_{p^*}^{\max}(t^{*-})
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Because $A_{p_{\text{succ}(p^*)}} \ne A_{p^*}$ satisfied $\text{Held}(A_{p_{\text{succ}(p^*)}}, t^{*-}) \ne \emptyset$ and $P_{\text{shared}} \in \mathcal{F}(T_{p^*}) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_{p_{\text{succ}(p^*)}})$, $A_{p_{\text{succ}(p^*)}} \in \mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{hold}}(A_{p^*}, t^{*-}) \subseteq \mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}(A_{p^*}, t^{*-})$. By the definition of the External Intersecting-Footprint System Ceiling (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ext_ceiling_def}), the external ceiling confronting $A_{p^*}$ at $t^{*-}$ satisfied:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ext_ceil_bound}
C_{-A_{p^*}}^*(t^{*-}; \mathcal{F}(T_{p^*})) \ge \Pi(P_{\text{shared}}, t^{*-}) \ge \Pi_{p^*}^{\max}(t^{*-})
\end{equation}
Simultaneously, because $A_{p^*}$ held no locks at $t^{*-}$ ($\text{Held}(A_{p^*}, t^{*-}) = \emptyset$), it had zero incoming wait edges ($\text{Waiters}(A_{p^*}, t^{*-}) = \emptyset$), so its effective priority equaled its instantaneous upper-envelope preemption level: $\Pi_{p^*}^{\text{eff}}(t^{*-}) = \Pi_{p^*}^{\max}(t^{*-})$. Moreover, because $\text{Held}(A_{p^*}, t^{*-}) = \emptyset$, the second disjunct of the EPCP Ceiling Admissibility Rule (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ceiling_rule}) evaluated to false at $t^{*-}$. Therefore, $A_{p^*}$ could be granted $R_{(p^*)}^{\text{first}}$ at $t^*$ if and only if the first disjunct of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ceiling_rule} held strictly:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:strict_dom}
\Pi_{p^*}^{\max}(t^{*-}) = \Pi_{p^*}^{\text{eff}}(t^{*-}) > C_{-A_{p^*}}^*(t^{*-}; \mathcal{F}(T_{p^*}))
\end{equation}
Combining Eq.~\eqref{eq:ext_ceil_bound} and Eq.~\eqref{eq:strict_dom} yields $\Pi_{p^*}^{\max}(t^{*-}) > C_{-A_{p^*}}^*(t^{*-}; \mathcal{F}(T_{p^*})) \ge \Pi_{p^*}^{\max}(t^{*-})$, i.e., $\Pi_{p^*}^{\max}(t^{*-}) > \Pi_{p^*}^{\max}(t^{*-})$, which is a direct mathematical contradiction. Thus, no cycle can ever form, and $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}(t)$ remains a strict DAG for all $t \ge 0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Theorem 2: Bounded Priority Inversion}
\begin{theorem}[Bounded Priority Inversion]
Under EPCP, a high-priority agent $A_H$ can be blocked by lower-preemption-level agents ($\Pi_L^{\max} < \Pi_H^{\max}$ with lower live urgency $\tilde{\Pi}_L^{\text{eff}}(t) < \tilde{\Pi}_H^{\text{eff}}(t)$) for at most the duration of a single critical section per mutually disjoint partition component in $\mathcal{F}(T_H)$ (at most one lower-priority lock holder across all mutually overlapping footprints, and at most $|\mathcal{F}(T_H)|$ concurrent lower-priority critical sections across disjoint partitions).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose agent $A_H$ requests its first lock at time $t$ with priority $\Pi_H^{\max}$ and is suspended under Section~III-C by a lower-priority agent $A_L$ ($\Pi_L^{\max} < \Pi_H^{\max}$) whose footprint intersects $\mathcal{F}(T_H)$ at shared partition $P_m \in \mathcal{F}(T_H) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_L)$ with $\Pi(P_m, t) \ge \Pi_H^{\max}$. First, among any set of lower-priority agents whose footprints overlap on $P_m$ (or overlap with one another), at most \textit{one} such lower-priority agent can hold a lock at time $t$: if two overlapping lower-priority agents $A_{L_1}$ and $A_{L_2}$ ($\mathcal{F}(T_{L_1}) \cap \mathcal{F}(T_{L_2}) \ne \emptyset$) both held locks, the second to enter ($t_{L_2}^{\text{entry}} > t_{L_1}^{\text{entry}}$) had $\text{Held}(A_{L_2}, t_{L_2}^{\text{entry}-}) = \emptyset$ and would have been required by Eq.~\eqref{eq:ceiling_rule} to satisfy $\Pi_{L_2}^{\max}(t_{L_2}^{\text{entry}-}) > C_{-A_{L_2}}^*(t_{L_2}^{\text{entry}-}; \mathcal{F}(T_{L_2})) \ge \Pi(P_m, t_{L_2}^{\text{entry}-}) \ge \Pi_{L_2}^{\max}(t_{L_2}^{\text{entry}-})$, which is impossible whenever $\mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}(A_{L_2}, t_{L_2}^{\text{entry}-}) \ne \emptyset$. Across at most $|\mathcal{F}(T_H)|$ mutually disjoint partitions, at most one pre-existing lower-priority holder per disjoint partition can exist at $A_H$'s arrival, and they execute concurrently in parallel. Furthermore, if $|\mathcal{F}(T_H)| \ge 2$ and one disjoint blocker $A_{L_1}$ on $P_1 \in \mathcal{F}(T_H)$ commits while $A_{L_2}$ on $P_2 \in \mathcal{F}(T_H)$ is still executing, $\textsc{ReleaseAllLocks}(A_{L_1})$ keeps $A_H$ bound to $A_{L_2}$ ($(A_H \to A_{L_2}) \in \mathcal{E}(t)$) and registers $A_H \in \mathcal{W}_{\text{pend}}(A_{L_3}, t)$ until $A_H$ acquires its first lock; by $\mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{queue}}(A_{L_3}, t)$ and $\mathcal{W}_{\text{pend}}(A_{L_3}, t)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ext_ceiling_def}, any newly arriving or woken lower-priority agent $A_{L_3}$ on $P_1$ ($\mathbf{\Pi}_{L_3}^{\text{eff}}(t) < \mathbf{\Pi}_H^{\text{eff}}(t)$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_{L_3}^{\text{eff}}(t) < \tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_H^{\text{eff}}(t)$) confronts $C_{-A_{L_3}}^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_{L_3})) \ge \Pi(P_1, t) \ge \Pi_H^{\max} > \Pi_{L_3}^{\text{eff}}(t)$ both while $A_H$ awaits $P_2$ and during the Mesa condition-variable handoff window after $A_{L_2}$ commits before $A_H$'s thread re-acquires the coordinator mutex.

Second, by Unified Transitive Priority Inheritance (Eqs.~\eqref{eq:inheritance}--\eqref{eq:inheritance_dyn}), each blocking holder $A_L$ immediately inherits $\Pi_L^{\text{eff}}(t) \ge \Pi_H^{\max}$ and $\tilde{\Pi}_L^{\text{eff}}(t) \ge \tilde{\Pi}_H^{\text{eff}}(t)$. Because $\text{Held}(A_L, t) \ne \emptyset$, single-holder admission exclusion across intersecting footprints (Eq.~\eqref{eq:disjoint_active_holders}) guarantees that $\mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}(A_L, t) = \emptyset$, so $C_{-A_L}^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_L)) = 0 < \Pi_L^{\text{eff}}(t)$ (and simultaneously $\Pi_L^{\text{eff}}(t) = C^*(t; \mathcal{F}(T_L))$ under Eq.~\eqref{eq:overall_ceiling_def}). Consequently, the first disjunct (as well as the second disjunct) of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ceiling_rule} allows $A_L$ to acquire any remaining Step-2 locks in $\mathcal{F}(T_L)$ without secondary ceiling self-blocking, complete its single critical section, and wake waiting threads in descending order of live dynamic urgency $\tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_k^{\text{eff}}(t)$ upon commit (where if another waiter $A_{L'}$ with lower static depth $d_{L'} < d_H$ is dispatched ahead of an unpromoted $A_H$ because $\tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_{L'}^{\text{eff}}(t) > \tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_H^{\text{eff}}(t)$ due to higher accumulated token/aging investment $\Omega_{L'}(t)$, $\mathcal{W}_{\text{pend}}$ permits $A_{L'}$'s admission and that dispatch reflects intentional epistemic sunk-cost scheduling under $\tilde{\Pi}^{\text{eff}}(t)$ rather than priority inversion).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Theorem 3: Conflict Serializability and Starvation-Free Progress}
\begin{theorem}[Serializability and Starvation Freedom]
Every execution schedule permitted under EPCP is conflict serializable, and every active transaction terminates in finite time without deadlock or starvation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First, consider conflict serializability. If a transaction $T_i$ ever requests an out-of-footprint resource $\text{part}(R_{\text{req}}) \,\notin\, \mathcal{F}(T_i)$ at Line 5 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:epcp}, its pre-expansion pass discards its uncommitted local write buffer with zero physical database mutations, releases all held locks, expands $\mathcal{F}(T_i) \leftarrow \mathcal{F}(T_i) \cup \{\text{part}(R_{\text{req}})\}$, and returns $\textsc{Restart}$ to re-execute from Step 1 ($\text{Held}(A_i, t) = \emptyset$). Since $|\mathcal{F}(T_i)| \in \{1, \dots, B_{\text{part}}\}$ strictly increases on each expansion, at most $B_{\text{part}} - 1 < 64$ transient $\textsc{Restart}$ transitions can occur. In the final committed pass, all locks are acquired during the growth phase and held until transaction commit or domain validation exit (Strict 2PL); moreover, if Step 1 replays a memoized $\text{Infer}_1(\phi)$ output from the local context cache, the SHA-256 \texttt{state\_version\_digest} check is evaluated \textit{after} $R_a$ is locked in the final pass, guaranteeing that $R_a$'s read attributes are bit-for-bit identical to the state on which $\text{Infer}_1(\phi)$ was computed. By Eswaran et al.~\cite{eswaran1976notions}, any schedule obeying strict two-phase locking is conflict serializable.

Second, by Theorem 1, $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}(t)$ is a strict DAG ($0$ deadlock aborts). To prove unconditional starvation freedom even under an unbounded continuous arrival stream of deeper ($d_j > d_i$) transactions where $\mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t)$ never empties, observe first that across the at most $B_{\text{part}} - 1 < 64$ transient $\textsc{Restart}$ transitions, $A_i$ remains in $\mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t)$ with preserved $(t_{\text{start},i}, \text{rank}(T_i))$ and immediately re-invokes $\textsc{AcquireLock}$ in bounded CPU time. In any pass where $T_i$ waits, Algorithm~\ref{alg:epcp} (Lines 10--12, 23, and 25--26) guarantees that $T_i$ either wakes via its bounded timeout at $t_{\text{start},i} + T_{\text{sat}}$ or is refreshed during $\textsc{ReleaseAllLocks}$, atomically promoting \textit{both} $\tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}_i(t)$ and $\mathbf{\Pi}_i^{\max}$ to the exact 64-bit integer lexicographical pair $\big(\lfloor 10^6 \Pi_{\text{sat}} \rfloor,\, -\text{rank}(T_i)\big)$, recomputing $\Pi(P_m, t)$ for all $P_m \in \mathcal{F}(T_i)$, and propagating the promoted priority along $\mathcal{G}_{\text{wait}}(t)$ via Eqs.~\eqref{eq:inheritance}--\eqref{eq:inheritance_dyn}. Because the coordinator compares exact 64-bit integer tuples $\big(\lfloor 10^6 \Pi_{\text{sat}} \rfloor,\, -\text{rank}(T_i)\big) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0} \times \mathbb{Z}_{< 0}$ and arrival ranks are strictly increasing with arrival time, $-\text{rank}(T_i) > -\text{rank}(T_j)$ holds for every transaction $T_j$ arriving after $T_i$ ($\text{rank}(T_i) < \text{rank}(T_j)$). Furthermore, by upward induction on $\text{rank}(T_q)$ starting from the oldest active transaction $T_{\min} = \arg\min_{T_q \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{active}}(t)} \text{rank}(T_q)$: any active lock holder $A_k$ currently holding a partition in $\mathcal{F}(T_{\min})$ has $\text{Held}(A_k, t) \ne \emptyset$ and by Eq.~\eqref{eq:disjoint_active_holders} acquires its remaining in-footprint locks without secondary ceiling blocking and finishes its critical section in bounded LLM/SQL steps; once $T_{\min}$ reaches $t_{\text{start},\min} + T_{\text{sat}}$, its promoted tuple $(\lfloor 10^6 \Pi_{\text{sat}} \rfloor, -\text{rank}(T_{\min}))$ strictly dominates every other active or newly arriving transaction under both $\mathbf{\Pi}^{\text{eff}}$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{\text{eff}}$, and $\mathcal{K}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{queue}}$ together with $\mathcal{W}_{\text{pend}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ext_ceiling_def} prevents any transaction $T_j$ ($\text{rank}(T_j) > \text{rank}(T_{\min})$) from entering any freed partition $P_m \in \mathcal{F}(T_{\min})$ ahead of $T_{\min}$ while $T_{\min}$ awaits its remaining partitions or wakes from $\text{CV}(A_{\min})$. Thus $T_{\min}$ acquires all locks and commits in finite time, inductively advancing every subsequent transaction $T_i$ to completion in finite time.
\end{proof}

\section{Empirical Evaluation and Benchmark Results}

\subsection{Experimental Architecture and Methodology}
We evaluated EPCP across two primary benchmark regimes plus a 3-table footprint-expansion sensitivity study on an Apple Silicon M-series workstation (8 CPU cores, APFS storage) fronting a local relational SQLite WAL storage engine (\texttt{PRAGMA journal\_mode = WAL; PRAGMA synchronous = NORMAL; PRAGMA busy\_timeout = 60000;}). To prevent SQLite's file-level single-writer lock from artificially serializing multi-step reasoning phases while guaranteeing zero dirty or stale reads, the prototype coordinator maintains a synchronized in-memory mirror of committed row state alongside the persistent SQLite WAL audit ledger: all row- and partition-level lock arbitration, condition-variable suspension ($\text{CV}(A_i)$), Step-1 \texttt{Biz-Rule Reject} checks, and intermediate reasoning steps ($\text{Infer}(\phi)$) read the exact latest committed state under held locks and stage mutations in a transaction-local write buffer; once all locks in $T_i$ are held and validated, the final physical SQLite mutation batch (\texttt{BEGIN IMMEDIATE; UPDATE ...; COMMIT;}) flushes to the WAL journal and updates the committed mirror atomically in $\approx 50\text{--}150\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ at the $\text{Commit}$ step. In Regime 2 (where throughput is $\le 20\text{ TPS}$), this $50\text{--}150\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ commit flush represents $< 0.3\%$ wall-time utilization, whereas in Regime 1 (where peak goodput reaches $673.85\text{--}1{,}075.45\text{ TPS}$), global single-writer WAL flush serialization accounts for $\approx 5\%\text{--}16\%$ of wall-clock time equally across all compared protocols (an ablation disabling the physical SQLite WAL flush in Regime 1 increases pure in-memory committed goodput at $K=20$ from $673.85\text{ TPS}$ to $768.40\text{ TPS}$ for EPCP and from $559.95\text{ TPS}$ to $628.10\text{ TPS}$ for S2PL, widening EPCP's relative goodput advantage from $+20.3\%$ to $+22.3\%$):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Regime 1 (High-Frequency Lock-Manager Stress Test, Table~\ref{tab:enterprise_telemetry}):} Isolates raw lock-table arbitration and ceiling condition-variable overhead using in-critical-section CPU-bound cryptographic HMAC-SHA256 policy verification and polynomial risk scoring ($\approx 0.5\text{--}2.0\text{ ms}$ per lock step, $|\mathcal{F}(T_i)| = 2$) across $K \in \{5, 10, 20, 30, 40\}$ concurrent worker threads ($25$ transactions/thread, $125$ to $1{,}000$ transactions/run, repeated across 5 independent random seeds). Prior to each $(K, \text{Protocol})$ run, the database is freshly re-seeded from a canonical snapshot of 10,000 Customer Accounts (initial balances $\$2{,}500\text{--}\$15{,}000$) and 5,000 Inventory Items (initial stock $15\text{--}45\text{ units/SKU}$, where each settlement deducts a depth-correlated lot quantity $q_i = 1 + 2d_i \in \{3, 5, 7, 9\}$ units and corresponding settlement value $\$150\text{--}\$950$) under heavy Zipfian contention ($\theta = 0.85$, concentrating 85\% of accesses on the top 50 hot accounts and top 30 hot inventory items, supplemented by a Zipfian skew sensitivity sweep across $\theta \in \{0.0, 0.50, 0.70, 0.85, 0.99\}$ in Section~V-B). In addition to the standard finite-stock snapshot (where hot SKUs naturally experience stock depletion at $K \ge 20$), we execute \textbf{Zero-Depletion Controls across $K \in \{20, 30, 40\}$} with $100\times$ initial balances/stock so $0$ \texttt{Biz-Rule Rejects} occur and 100\% of non-deadlocked transactions execute full 2-step settlements.
    \item \textbf{Regime 2 (High-Latency 500~ms--1,500~ms 2-Step LLM Reasoning Benchmark \& Live Vertex AI Evaluation, Table~\ref{tab:llm_regime}):} Evaluates 2-step agent transactions ($\text{Lock}(R_a) \to \text{Infer}_1(\phi) \to \text{Lock}(R_b) \to \text{Infer}_2(\phi) \to \text{Commit}$, with $25$ transactions/thread $= 500$ attempts at $K=20$ and $1{,}000$ attempts at $K=40$ per seed across 5 seeds on the $100\times$ Zero-Depletion snapshot, $|\mathcal{F}(T_i)| = 2$ over $B_{\text{part}}=64$, $d_i \sim \text{Uniform}\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$, initial $\mathcal{H}_i \sim \text{Beta}(2, 5)$, $800\text{ non-preemptible LLM tokens}$ billed per $\text{Infer}$ step $= 1{,}600\text{ tokens}$ per full 2-step attempt, and uniform $250\text{--}750\text{ ms}$ reasoning latency per step $= 500\text{--}1{,}500\text{ ms}$ total 2-step in-lock reasoning duration per transaction, mean $1.00\text{ s}$, giving a Little's Law zero-wait upper bound of $20.0\text{ Tx/s}$ at $K=20$ and $40.0\text{ Tx/s}$ at $K=40$). Table~\ref{tab:llm_regime} reports single-pass attempt metrics alongside cumulative token waste per $1{,}000$ committed workflows under both the stationary geometric projection $\frac{\text{Attempt Waste}}{1 - r_{\text{abort}}}$ and closed-loop timestamp-preserving retry execution (where aborted transactions preserve their initial birth timestamp $t_{\text{start},i}$ across retries as required by Rosenkrantz et al.~\cite{rosenkrantz1978system} until all $1{,}000$ workflows commit). We compare 11 protocols and ablations spanning all canonical concurrency control families~\cite{harding2017evaluation}: \textbf{S2PL}~\cite{eswaran1976notions}, \textbf{2PL-Wait Promote}~\cite{abbott1992scheduling} (applying transitive epistemic priority inheritance $\tilde{\Pi}_j^{\text{eff}}(t)$ on direct row contention without EPCP's ceiling admission gate), \textbf{Wound-Wait}~\cite{rosenkrantz1978system, corbett2013spanner}, \textbf{Wait-Die}~\cite{rosenkrantz1978system}, \textbf{OCC (Snapshot Read + CAS Commit)}~\cite{kung1981optimistic}, \textbf{Coarse Shard-Scoped C2PL (\texttt{Shard-C2PL}, $B_{\text{shard}}=8$)}~\cite{bernstein1987concurrency}, \textbf{Fine-Grained Partition C2PL (\texttt{Part-C2PL}, $B_{\text{part}}=64$, FIFO)} (acquiring all partitions in $\mathcal{F}(T_i)$ atomically upfront with FIFO wait queues), \textbf{64-Partition Canonical Ordered Locking (\texttt{Part-Canonical}, $B_{\text{part}}=64$)} (acquiring partition locks sequentially in ascending order $\min(\mathcal{F}(T_i)) < \max(\mathcal{F}(T_i))$ prior to Step 1 without a speculative pre-pass), \textbf{Row-Level Canonical Ordered Locking (Discovery Pre-Pass)} (executing an unlocked speculative $\text{Infer}_1$ pre-pass to discover $R_b$, sorting $\min(R_a, R_b) < \max(R_a, R_b)$, and re-running $\text{Infer}_1$ if $R_a$ mutated prior to lock acquisition), \textbf{EPCP Ablation (Static $\Pi_i^{\max}$ Only, w/o Live $\tilde{\Pi}_i(t)$)}, and \textbf{Full EPCP}. Finally, we supplement the controlled sweep with a \textbf{Live Google Cloud Vertex AI (\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash}) Multi-Agent Evaluation} across $K \in \{10, 20\}$ concurrent worker threads ($3$ independent seeds across S2PL, Wound-Wait, OCC, EPCP Static-$\Pi_i^{\max}$, and Full EPCP on the $100\times$ Zero-Depletion snapshot).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Analysis of Empirical Telemetry}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Total Elimination of Deadlocks and Token Waste Across All 11 Protocols and Ablations:} Across all concurrency levels in Table~\ref{tab:enterprise_telemetry} and Table~\ref{tab:llm_regime} (and Figure~\ref{fig:abort_rate}), EPCP recorded \textbf{0 deadlocks} ($0.00\%$ abort rate) and \textbf{0 wasted LLM tokens}. In Regime 2 ($K=40$, Table~\ref{tab:llm_regime}), S2PL suffered $18.40\%$ deadlock aborts at Lock 2 ($147.2\text{k}$ wasted tokens per $1{,}000$ attempts / $180.4\text{k}$ per $1{,}000$ committed workflows). Notably, \texttt{2PL-Wait Promote}~\cite{abbott1992scheduling}---which includes EPCP's epistemic priority inheritance on direct row contention but omits the partition ceiling admission gate---still suffered $16.20\%$ WFG deadlock aborts ($129.6\text{k}$ wasted tokens/1k attempts), proving that priority inheritance alone cannot prevent multi-resource cycles without ceiling admission control. \section{Conclusion}
This paper presented the Epistemic Priority Ceiling Protocol (EPCP) for centralized shared-memory transactional lock coordinators and connection-pool gateways fronting enterprise OLTP databases. By combining exact 64-bit integer-tuple epistemic priorities ($\Pi_i^{\max}$ and $\tilde{\Pi}_i(t)$), 64-partition intersecting-footprint priority ceilings, guarded preemptive admission checks, and unified transitive priority inheritance, EPCP guarantees that the Unified Wait-For-Graph remains a strict DAG at all times and achieves $0.00\%$ deadlock aborts across sub-millisecond stress tests, multi-step LLM reasoning benchmarks, 3-table dynamic footprint-expansion workloads, and live Vertex AI \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} multi-agent evaluations over an in-memory row-state mirror backed by SQLite WAL. In distributed OLTP deployments such as Cloud Spanner, AlloyDB, or CockroachDB where commit-phase Paxos/Raft consensus adds $5\text{--}15\text{ ms}$ to lock hold durations (still $<1\%\text{--}3\%$ of a $500\text{--}5{,}000\text{ ms}$ multi-step agent reasoning window), EPCP's non-aborting ceiling arbitration similarly prevents consensus-amplified rollback thrashing. Future work will extend EPCP's ceiling coordination across decentralized multi-leader two-phase commit (2PC) consensus groups, fine-grained phase- and record-adaptive agentic concurrency controllers, and standard multi-warehouse OLTP suites such as TPC-C.
